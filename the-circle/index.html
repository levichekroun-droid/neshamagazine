Interviews

The interview I was told was almost impossible.

One interview is published so far. A second spot is saved for the next one.

Published in Issue 12

Boruch Perlowitz

I watched Boruch Perlowitz for years, even before NeshamaGazine existed, and I wanted to interview him so badly. My father told me, very nicely, that it was almost impossible, because Boruch is a super busy guy.

"Nah. It's possible."

So my father messaged him on LinkedIn. Days passed. Weeks passed. I went to overnight camp and forgot about it, until a message came from my father: Boruch answered. When I got home I called him myself and asked if I could interview him. He said, "Sure."

The interview ran in our 68-page Mega Rosh Hashana issue, which went to 15 shuls.

My Story

It all started with a frying pan.

How a comic I never finished turned into a magazine. By Levi Chekroun, Publisher and Editor in Chief.

01

A frying pan

In 2024, my grandmother gave me a script for a comic called The Third Trapdoor. It was about October 7th, which was not funny at all. But the story had a hero moment: terrorists were breaking into a family's house, and the father grabbed the first thing he could find, a frying pan full of sizzling oil.

I asked ChatGPT to draw the whole comic. It said it couldn't make an entire comic book, but it gave me a clue: people make pictures like that on Canva. So I opened Canva, and I kind of never closed it.

02

A name on a Shabbos walk

On a Shabbos walk with my mother, I said, "Ma, we need a name." My mother is a designer, and inventing names is her job. Neshama plus magazine equals NeshamaGazine. It was done before we got home.

03

The first try

My very first magazine was not good. So not good that it never got an issue number. I printed it, my family read it, and they said they liked it. That's what families are for.

04

Issue 1: Escape to Shanghai

For the next one I wanted a real feature. In a Jewish magazine my mother gave me, I found the true story of the escape to Shanghai. I wrote it up in day camp, when I was around nine or ten. Lots of people took a copy. Nobody went crazy about it. Yet.

05

The issue that changed everything

Then my mother told me the true story of Eli Cohen, the spy who lived a double life. I turned it into our Rosh Hashana issue, printed 14 copies and put them in my shul. Everyone took one. After davening, people came over and said, "Wow, this is so good!" From that day on, people knew to grab NeshamaGazine.

06

One kid became a team

Around Issue 5, I brought copies to school and the kids loved it. Two of them asked to join. Best decision ever, theirs and mine. On Lag BaOmer I brought Issue 8 for the parade, and before the parade the whole school was reading it.

07

48 pages, then 68

For Shavuos we went all in and pulled off a 48-page mega issue. We had never made anything that big. In Issue 10, our own characters, Shimmy and Mimmy, appeared on the cover.

Then came the interview with Boruch Perlowitz and our 68-page Mega Rosh Hashana issue. My mother helped design it, and we put it in 15 different shuls, three copies each.

08

And now

Issue 13 is out. There is a website with a reader, an arcade and a robot. The next issue is Chanukah. Stay tuned.
